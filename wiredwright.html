What the research says, before you write a word

      
Eleven findings from the landing-page and local-search research behind these questions. They are the reason the questions are the questions.

      

        

          
5–15% conversion

          
A landing page built for one job, on traffic that came looking for that job. This is the whole argument for building the page at all.

          ResultCalls; ServiceTitan
        

        

          
+14%

          
Naming the credential beats claiming it. “Licensed Master Electrician, TECL #…” outperforms a generic “licensed and insured” badge.

          Web Tonic
        

        

          
56% of 1,200+ sites

          
A 2024 audit of electrician websites found most hide their license number, and 42% never mention being licensed or insured at all. The bar is low.

          BuiltRight Digital
        

        

          
74% mobile

          
Three quarters of electrical-service traffic arrives on a phone, often mid-problem. The phone layout is the layout.

          Web Tonic
        

        

          
+15–25%

          
Moving the tap-to-call button into a sticky mobile header is the single highest-leverage layout change vendors report.

          Web Tonic
        

        

          
68% hire first responder

          
Most home-service customers go with whoever answers first, not whoever is best. Speed beats polish.

          Scorpion
        

        

          
+10–18%

          
A real job photo in the hero outperforms stock imagery. A rebuilt panel or a before/after shot does more work than a model in a toolbelt.

          Web Tonic
        

        

          
60–70% form abandonment

          
Contractor forms lose most of the people who start them, and multi-step forms perform worst. Four fields: name, phone, service type, urgency.

          Web Tonic
        

        

          
28% vs 65% told

          
J.D. Power found only 28% of EV owners genuinely needed panel work, while 65% were told they did. Saying so out loud is a trust play competitors will not copy.

          ChargeRight, citing J.D. Power
        

        

          
$1,200 Austin Energy rebate

          
Austin Energy pays 50% of charger plus labor, up to $1,200 for an OCPP smart charger. It requires a licensed electrician and, for hardwired units, a City of Austin permit and inspection. Verify current terms before publishing.

          Austin Energy
        

        

          
47 reviews

          
Top-ranking local businesses average roughly 47 Google reviews. Volume tends to beat a perfect score: 200 at 4.6 outranks 30 at 5.0 on competitive queries.

          Rank Planners

Where the numbers come from

    
Every conversion figure quoted on this page comes from marketing-agency or vendor blogs, not peer-reviewed research or independently audited data. Treat them as directional, not settled. The Austin Energy rebate and permit terms come from Austin Energy's own program pages and should be re-checked before you print them on a website, because program funding and caps change.
